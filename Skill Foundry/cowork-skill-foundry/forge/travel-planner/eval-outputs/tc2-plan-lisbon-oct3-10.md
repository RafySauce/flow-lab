### Day 2 — Saturday, October 4: Alfama & Fado

**Morning**
Breakfast at a pastelaria — try a pastel de nata. Flag nut allergy if anything is unfamiliar.
Walk or tuk-tuk up to **Castelo de São Jorge** — the Moorish hilltop castle. Views over the entire city. Go early before crowds build.

**Afternoon**
Wander **Alfama**, Lisbon's oldest neighborhood. Narrow streets, tiled buildings, viewpoints (miradouros) at every turn. Stop at Miradouro das Portas do Sol.
Lunch in Alfama — grilled sardines or bacalhau (salt cod) are the local staples. Always confirm: ask about sauces, dressings, shared fryers.

**Evening**
Fado dinner — this is the experience Lisbon is built around. Traditional fado houses (casas de fado) combine a set dinner with live fado music.
**⚠️ Book this reservation before departure — top houses fill weeks out.** Flag F1's allergy at the time of booking and again at the table.
Options worth researching: Clube de Fado (Alfama), Sr. Fado, Mesa de Frades — verify current availability and reputation before departure.

### Day 3 — Sunday, October 5: Belém

**Morning**
Take the tram or rideshare to **Belém**, the riverside district (~20 min from center).
- **Pastéis de Belém** — the original pastel de nata bakery. The tarts themselves are nut-free; ask about anything else.
- **Mosteiro dos Jerónimos** — Manueline monastery, UNESCO World Heritage.
- **Torre de Belém** — iconic river tower; worth the outside view even if you skip the interior queue.

**Afternoon**
Lunch in Belém — riverside restaurants. Grilled fish and seafood are straightforward for F1.
Return to center. **LX Factory** — creative market complex open Sundays. Food stalls, local design, books, a flea market. Great for souvenirs. F1: ask before tasting anything at open stalls — cross-contamination risk.

**Evening**
Dinner in **Chiado** or **Bairro Alto**. Both neighborhoods are Lisbon's best for restaurant density. Chiado skews upscale; Bairro Alto is more neighborhood-casual. Reserve in advance; flag allergy at booking.

### Day 4 — Monday, October 6: Sintra Day Trip

**Morning**
Day trip to **Sintra** — 40 min by train from Rossio station. Buy train tickets in advance or at the station. One of the most spectacular landscapes in Europe: palaces perched in forested hills, gardens, ruins.
- **Palácio Nacional da Pena** — colorful romantic palace; buy timed entry before departure
- **Quinta da Regaleira** — mystical estate with spiral wells and elaborate gardens
- **Moorish Castle ruins** — sweeping views; moderate hike

Wear comfortable walking shoes — Sintra involves hills and cobblestones.

**Afternoon**
Lunch in Sintra town. Simpler dishes (grilled meats, fish) are safer than sauces with unknown composition.
Return to Lisbon by mid-afternoon or early evening.

**Evening**
Low-key evening. Aperitivo in the hotel bar or a glass of wine in **Príncipe Real** neighborhood — quieter, elegant feel.

### Day 5 — Tuesday, October 7: Mouraria, Markets & Local Lisbon

**Morning**
Explore **Mouraria** — the Moorish neighborhood adjacent to Alfama; more local, less touristed. Small tascas (traditional lunch spots) throughout.
**Mercado da Ribeira (Time Out Market)** — covered food hall. Good for coffee or a snack; F1 should be selective and ask directly at vendor stalls.

**Afternoon**
**Museu Nacional do Azulejo** — the tile museum. Azulejos (decorative ceramic tiles) are central to Portuguese identity; the museum is genuinely beautiful. Budget 2 hours.
Coffee and pastelaria break. Afternoon browse in Príncipe Real — independent shops, a garden, antiques.

**Evening**
One of the best restaurant nights of the trip. Book a top-tier Lisbon table tonight.
Options worth researching: Alma (Michelin-starred), Bairro do Avillez, Cervejaria Ramiro (legendary seafood, no reservations — arrive early).
**⚠️ Verify current status and reservation procedures before departure — closures and chef changes happen.**
Kitchens at this level take allergies seriously; flag F1's allergy at booking and at the table.

### Day 6 — Wednesday, October 8: Arrábida or Free Day

**Option A — Arrábida Natural Park (day trip)**
~1 hour south of Lisbon by rental car. Dramatic limestone cliffs, turquoise water, hiking, unspoiled coastline. October water is still swimmable for the adventurous. Bring a picnic — pack nut-free snacks.

**Option B — Lisbon at your own pace**
Revisit anything you loved, shop (Rua Garrett in Chiado for high-end, Santa Catarina for independent boutiques), or sit in a miradouro with a glass of wine. Lisbon rewards slow time.

**Evening**
Dinner in a neighborhood you've spent less time in — Alcântara for something more local.

### Day 7 — Thursday, October 9: Queluz + Final Lisbon

**Morning**
**Queluz National Palace** — the "Portuguese Versailles," 30 min by train. Baroque gardens and formal rooms; lovely on a clear October morning. Often less crowded than Sintra.

**Afternoon**
Return to Lisbon. Final shopping and souvenir run. Pack tonight; buy wine at airport duty-free on the way home if desired.

**Evening**
Farewell dinner — make it a reservation-worthy night. Return to a favorite neighborhood or somewhere you didn't get to earlier in the week. Bring cash for tips. Early night — departure is tomorrow morning.

### F1 Allergy Protocol

**EpiPen location:** On F1's person at all times — not in a bag, not in checked luggage. Two pens if possible.

**Flights — every leg, outbound and return:**
Notify flight attendant at boarding: *"I have a severe peanut and tree nut allergy — please don't serve nut snacks near our row."*
Bring nut-free snacks for the flight; don't rely on airline options.

**Restaurants — every meal, every time:**
Flag at reservation when booking. Flag again at the table when seated — tell the server, ask them to tell the kitchen. Ask about sauces, dressings, desserts, and anything unfamiliar.

**Portuguese cuisine — allergy landscape:**
Traditional Portuguese food is relatively low-risk compared to Thai, Middle Eastern, or North African cuisines. The cuisine centers on olive oil, fish (especially bacalhau), seafood, pork, chicken, bread, and potatoes.

Primary caution areas:
- **Desserts and pastries** — some contain amêndoa (almonds), pinhões (pine nuts), or avelã (hazelnut). The pastel de nata itself is nut-free; ask about everything else.
- **Sauces with unknown composition** — ask about every sauce
- **Street food and open-air market stalls** — cross-contamination risk is elevated; favor sit-down restaurants
- **LX Factory food stalls** — exercise caution; ask before tasting

**Translation card — print multiple copies, carry in wallet and bag:**

> *"Tenho uma alergia grave a amendoins e nozes (incluindo amêndoas, cajus, nozes, avelãs, pistácios e pinhões). Esta é uma emergência médica potencialmente fatal. Por favor, certifique-se de que a minha refeição não contém estes ingredientes nem foi preparada em superfícies ou utensílios que os tenham tocado. Obrigado/a."*

**Translation:** *"I have a severe allergy to peanuts and tree nuts (including almonds, cashews, walnuts, hazelnuts, pistachios, and pine nuts). This is a potentially fatal medical emergency. Please ensure my meal does not contain these ingredients and was not prepared on surfaces or with utensils that touched them. Thank you."*
