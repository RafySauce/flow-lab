# Writing Style — CV and cover letter prose rules

Harvested nearly intact from the source's `03-writing-style.md` — the strongest
single file in the foreign material. Read before any drafting.

## Critical rules

1. **No em-dashes.** Commas, periods, or restructure.
2. **No clichés or filler.** Cut: "passionate about," "great fit," "leverage my
   skills," "hit the ground running," "drive results," "synergies."
3. **No buzzwords without concrete backing.** Every claim carries a specific
   example or fact.
4. **No apologetic language.** Not "I think I could contribute" but "I bring X,
   demonstrated by Y."
5. **No unverified company claims.** Every company-specific statement
   (partnerships, products, technology, expansions) is independently verified via
   live search before inclusion. Reviewer-agent research is never trusted at face
   value. Unverifiable → generalize or omit.
6. **Reframe emphasis, not substance — the interview backtrack test.** Could the
   candidate explain this bullet in an interview without backtracking? If they'd
   have to say "well, what I actually meant was…", it's too far.
   - **OK:** reordering to lead with relevance; natural synonyms for the target
     domain; emphasizing one aspect of a broad role.
   - **Flag it:** combining academic + industry experience into a claim implying
     all-industry; using the posting's exact terminology for work that was
     adjacent, not the same. → Present to the user: "this bullet is a stretch
     because X — keep, soften, or drop?"
   - **Never:** claiming experience that doesn't exist; implying a domain never
     worked in.

## Tone

Warm but direct. Conversational professional — how a confident person talks in a
good interview. First person, active voice. Demonstrate, don't state: a specific
teamwork example beats "I am a team player."

Voice must match the person: write to the profile's self-assessment register. A
collaborative profile doesn't get solo-hero prose; a direct one doesn't get
hedged apologies.

## Cover letter structure (~1 page, hard rule)

- **Opening:** the role and why, 1 sentence; immediate background-to-role
  connection, 1–2 sentences. Specific, never a template opener.
- **Why this company (early):** the first section after the opening. Their goals,
  their language, what the candidate contributes to them — not what the candidate
  gains. Reference any real conversation with someone there.
- **Body — task-solving focus:** which of *their* tasks the candidate can solve
  and how; methods and tools they'll bring; 3–5 specific, outcome-oriented
  bullets where useful; 1–2 past examples only as backing for forward-looking
  claims. The letter is not a CV repetition.
- **Closing:** brief, confident, forward-looking. No begging.

**Headline formula:** [title/specialty] + [relevant keyword from the posting] —
never "Application for X Position."

## Scannability

Descriptive subheadings, posting keywords where natural, no filler. Employers
scan first and read second; structure for the scan.

## Language

Match the posting's language; a letter in another language should feel written,
not translated. CV stays in English unless the market dictates otherwise.

## Per-role-type emphasis

- **Technical:** lead with languages, frameworks, architectures; data volumes;
  independent projects.
- **Domain-specific:** lead with domain expertise and methods; technical skills
  framed as amplifiers of domain work.
- **Consulting/advisory:** lead with stakeholder communication and
  technical-business bridging.
- **Leadership/senior:** lead with project leadership, mentoring; advanced
  degrees framed as independent delivery.
