# Interview Prep — STAR framework, tough questions, roleplay

Harvested from the source's `07-interview-prep.md`. Examples are built from the
person's career profile at prep time — nothing canned, nothing invented.

## STAR format

**Situation** (context) → **Task** (their responsibility) → **Action** (what they
did, specifically) → **Result** (measurable outcome). 1–2 minutes per answer; end
with what was learned. Build 4–6 examples per person covering distinct
competencies, each tagged with the question types it answers. Store them in the
person's career profile once written, so they accumulate across applications.

## Tough questions to prep (answers drafted from the profile, confirmed with the user)

- "Why did you leave X?" — honest, forward-looking, zero negativity about the
  former employer.
- "You don't have [skill]." — acknowledge, bridge to adjacent experience, show
  the learning path.
- "Where in 5 years?" — ambition aligned with this role's actual growth path.
- "Biggest weakness?" — a real one, with a concrete mitigation.
- "Why this company?" — must reference specifics (projects, values, market
  position) verified during the application research. Never generic.

## Questions the candidate should ask

- **Role:** typical week; success at 6 months; team's biggest current challenge.
- **Team:** size and division of work; lifecycle idea→production; onboarding.
- **Growth:** stack for the relevant area; room toward
  architectural/strategic work; how the team stays current.
- **Culture (disappointment-prevention):** team culture in their words;
  development-vs-maintenance balance; leadership style; remote/hybrid
  flexibility; what people who thrive there have in common.

## Roleplay protocol

1. Confirm role and company to simulate.
2. Warm-up ("tell me about yourself") → role-specific technical → 1–2 behavioral
   drawn from the posting's competencies → one curveball.
3. After each answer: brief feedback — what worked, what to sharpen, which STAR
   example would have served better.

## Logistics tips (small, real)

Have the STAR examples written out and visible. Water nearby. Smile when
speaking — it changes tone. Five seconds of thought before answering is fine.
Close with "anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the application

Don't call "to stand out" post-submission. Respect stated timelines; after 2+
silent weeks with no stated timeline, a brief status call is acceptable. Any
update received (interview, rejection, status) earns a 2–3 sentence thank-you.
